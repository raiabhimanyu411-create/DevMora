Daily checks

Turn a pre-start checklist into a clear driver workflow

A digital checklist is useful when it fits the work and creates a clear route for defects to reach the office.

A pre-start app can give each driver a vehicle-specific daily check on a phone or tablet. The driver selects the truck, trailer or item of plant, works through the relevant visual inspection points and records a result. If a fault is found, the driver can describe it, attach a photo and send it to the supervisor or maintenance team.

For operators, the value comes from having a consistent record and a visible follow-up process. A dashboard can show which checks have been submitted, which defects are waiting for review and whether a repair or other action has been recorded. Reports can be searched by vehicle, driver, depot or date, reducing the time spent reconciling paper sheets.

Different operations need different checks. A linehaul truck, a vehicle combination, a tipper and a piece of construction plant may have different inspection prompts and escalation paths. A custom app can show the relevant workflow for each asset and let the operator maintain the checklist it has approved.

  • Defect descriptions and photo capture
  • Driver acknowledgement and timestamps
  • Supervisor notification and repair status
  • Offline capture with later synchronization
  • Searchable records and operational exports
Australian guidance

Adapt the daily check to your operation

NHVR guidance describes daily checks as quick visual inspections and says operators can tailor the guide to their circumstances.

The National Heavy Vehicle Regulator’s daily-check guide lists areas such as brakes, couplings, wheels and tyres. It describes a daily check as a visual inspection before leaving a yard, depot or rest area, and says the guide can be expanded and tailored to an operator’s business. It also says the guide is not a replacement for a full safety inspection.

That makes configurability important. The app should use the operator’s own approved checklist, provide instructions for items that need an additional person or equipment, and route faults to someone who can assess them. A digital form is a recordkeeping and workflow tool; it should not be presented as a substitute for training, maintenance or a complete inspection.

The Heavy Vehicle National Law applies in participating jurisdictions, while arrangements can differ elsewhere. We scope the deployment around where the vehicles operate and the guidance that applies to the business, then have the operator’s safety lead validate the checklist before it goes live.

From defect to repair

Make follow-up visible to the maintenance team

A check should not disappear into an inbox once the driver presses submit.

When a driver reports an issue, the app can notify a fleet manager and create a review task. The manager records an assessment, decides what action is needed, assigns work and documents when it is complete. The report remains connected to the vehicle history, so repeated issues are easier to see and discuss.

A mobile app should also handle the realities of regional work. If the connection drops, a check can be saved on the device and synchronized later. The driver needs a clear state showing whether the report is local, queued or delivered. The office needs timestamps and a way to detect a delayed report without relying on memory.

Where the client uses a TMS or maintenance package, an integration can pass vehicle and defect information into that system. We confirm available APIs, source-of-truth rules and error handling before promising a connection. That keeps the inspection app aligned with the tools the team already depends on.

Project scope

Choose the right first release for the fleet

Start with one depot or vehicle group, then expand after drivers and maintenance staff have used it.

Discovery maps the current inspection form, who completes it, who reviews faults and where repairs are recorded. We identify asset categories, languages, offline needs, user roles and the reporting the business actually needs. Those details determine the screens, data model and integration plan.

The first release might include the driver checklist, defect photos, supervisor review and a basic vehicle history. Later releases can add job data, maintenance scheduling, document expiry reminders or a full transport management system. Each stage has a written scope and working demonstrations on a staging site.

Custom development is most useful when off-the-shelf products cannot follow your operation or connect to the tools you use. If an existing app already fits, using it may be simpler. We will compare the requirements before recommending a build.
