How to Outsource Web Development to India Without the Usual Headaches (2026 Guide)

A practical 2026 guide to outsourcing web and app development to India: how to choose a partner, structure the project, communicate across time zones and protect your code and IP.

Outsourcing web development to India can save a growing business months of hiring time and a large share of its budget. It can also go badly wrong. The difference is rarely the country or the hourly rate. It is almost always how the partner is chosen and how the project is run.

This guide is written from the other side of the table. At Devmora Technology we build web apps, SaaS products and mobile apps for clients in the UK, Canada and beyond from our base in India, so we see what makes these projects succeed and what quietly sinks them.

Why companies outsource to India

The obvious reason is cost, but it is not the only one. The teams that get the most value usually outsource for three reasons together:

  • Speed to start. Hiring a senior developer locally can take months. An established outsourcing partner can usually start within a couple of weeks.
  • Breadth of skills. A small team often covers front end, back end, mobile, DevOps and SEO, which would otherwise mean several separate hires.
  • Time-zone leverage. With a good handover routine, work progresses while your own team is offline, so you wake up to updates rather than blockers.

Common problems, and why they happen

Most outsourcing horror stories come from a handful of root causes:

  • Vague scope. "Build us an app like X" leaves too much room for interpretation, so the result does not match what was in your head.
  • No visibility. You only see the product at the end, when changes are expensive.
  • Account-manager layers. Your questions pass through people who do not write the code, and answers come back slow and diluted.
  • Code you cannot maintain. No documentation, no tests, and repositories held by the vendor.

Every one of these is preventable with the right partner and a clear process.

Choosing the right partner

Ignore the polished slide deck and look for evidence. When we talk to new clients, these are the questions we encourage them to ask any vendor, including us:

  1. Can I see live products you built? Not mock-ups: real URLs or apps you can open today.
  2. Who will actually write my code? Ask to speak to the developers, not only a sales contact.
  3. How do you show progress? The right answer involves a staging link and regular demos.
  4. Who owns the repository? You should, from day one.
  5. What happens after launch? Look for a clear bug-fix period and an optional maintenance plan.
Tip: start with a small, paid first milestone. It is the cheapest way to test communication, code quality and reliability before committing to a large build.

Structuring the project

Good outsourced projects are broken into short, visible milestones. A structure that works well for most web apps:

  1. Discovery (1 to 2 weeks). Goals, users, key flows and a written scope that both sides sign off.
  2. Design. Wireframes, then high-fidelity screens for the main journeys, agreed before development starts.
  3. Build in sprints. One or two-week cycles, each ending with a demo on a live staging link.
  4. Launch. Deployment, monitoring, analytics and basic SEO set up properly from day one.
  5. Support. A bug-fix window, then an optional monthly plan for updates and new features.

Fixed-scope projects work best when requirements are clear. If your product is still evolving, a dedicated-developer arrangement gives you more flexibility.

Communication across time zones

India is ahead of the UK by four and a half to five and a half hours, and ahead of North America by nine and a half to thirteen and a half hours, depending on daylight saving. That overlap is enough for a short daily or weekly call if both sides plan for it.

  • Agree one shared channel (Slack, Teams or WhatsApp) and one place for tasks (Jira, Linear, Trello or GitHub Issues).
  • Ask for a short written update at the end of each working day: what was done, what is next, what is blocked.
  • Keep decisions in writing, even if they were made on a call.

Protecting your code and IP

  • Sign an NDA before sharing sensitive details, and a contract that assigns all IP to you.
  • Host the code in your GitHub or GitLab organisation and add the vendor as collaborators.
  • Keep production credentials, domains and cloud accounts in your name.
  • Ask for a short README and deployment notes as part of every milestone.
