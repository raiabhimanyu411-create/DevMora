Transport Management System for UK Hauliers: The Features That Actually Matter

What should a transport management system (TMS) do for a small or mid-sized UK haulier? Jobs, fleet compliance, defect tracking, MOT and document reminders, driver apps and reporting explained.

Many small and mid-sized UK haulage firms still run on a mix of spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups and paper defect sheets. It works until it does not: a missed MOT, a lost defect report or an expired driver document can turn into a fine, a prohibition or a difficult conversation with the Traffic Commissioner.

We built a transport management system (TMS) and companion driver app for a UK haulage business, so this guide focuses on the features that make a measurable difference day to day.

Why spreadsheets stop working

  • Information lives in several places, so nobody has the full picture.
  • Reminders depend on someone remembering to check a sheet.
  • Paper defect reports get lost, are hard to read and are slow to act on.
  • Pulling records together for an audit takes days instead of minutes.

Jobs, planning and dispatch

At its core, a TMS should let your office team create jobs, assign vehicles and drivers, and see job status in real time. Look for:

  • Job creation with collection and delivery details, references and notes
  • Vehicle and driver assignment with clash warnings
  • Live status updates from the driver app
  • Proof of delivery with photos or signatures

Fleet compliance built in

Under the DVSA Guide to maintaining roadworthiness, operators are expected to have an effective system for daily walkaround checks, defect reporting and preventative maintenance, with records kept so they can be inspected. A good TMS turns those expectations into a simple workflow:

  • Digital walkaround checks completed in the driver app before the vehicle is used
  • Defect-to-repair tracking, so every reported defect has a clear owner, status and sign-off
  • Inspection scheduling for safety inspections at your planned intervals
  • Vehicle reminders for MOT, road tax, insurance and inspection due dates

Driver documents and reminders

Driving licences, Driver CPC qualification cards, tachograph cards and medicals all expire. A TMS should store expiry dates and send reminders well in advance, so nobody is sent out on a job with an out-of-date document.

A driver app that drivers will use

The best compliance system fails if drivers find it awkward. The driver app should be fast, simple and work with weak signal:

  • Large buttons and a short, guided walkaround checklist
  • Photo capture for defects, attached automatically to the vehicle record
  • Today's jobs with one-tap status updates
  • Offline mode that syncs when the connection returns

Read more in our guide to digital walkaround checks.

Reports for audits and customers

When DVSA, a customer or an accreditation scheme asks for evidence, you should be able to export walkaround history, defect records and inspection reports for any vehicle and date range in a few clicks.

Off-the-shelf or custom?

Off-the-shelf TMSCustom TMS
Speed to startFastWeeks to a few months
Fit to your workflowYou adapt to the softwareSoftware fits how you work
Ongoing costPer-vehicle or per-user feesBuild cost plus hosting and support
IntegrationsLimited to what is offeredBuilt around your systems

For many operators, off-the-shelf software is the right start. Custom software makes sense when your workflow is unusual, you run several connected businesses, or per-vehicle fees are adding up. Talk to us if you want an honest view on which fits your fleet, or see the transport management system we built.

This article is general guidance, not legal advice. Always check current DVSA guidance and your operator licence undertakings.
