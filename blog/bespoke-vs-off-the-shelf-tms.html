Bespoke vs Off-the-Shelf TMS: Which Fits Your Fleet?

Should a haulier buy a ready-made transport management system or have one built? When each makes sense, how to compare the real costs, and what a first bespoke version should include.

By Abhimanyu RaiPublished 20 November 20265 min read

  2. When off-the-shelf is the right call

Every haulier who outgrows spreadsheets hits the same question: buy a ready-made transport management system (TMS), or have one built? Both answers are right for someone. The wrong answer costs you either years of paying for software that does not fit, or a build budget spent on features you could have rented.

We built a bespoke TMS and driver app for a UK haulage business that is now in daily use, so this guide is written from the build side. It is also honest about when you should not build.

What each option actually means

Off-the-shelf TMS is software you subscribe to. The vendor builds one product for many operators, hosts it and updates it. You configure it, but you work the way the product works. Pricing is usually per vehicle, per driver or per user, every month.

Bespoke TMS is software built for your operation. It covers the jobs, checks and reports you need and nothing else. You pay for the build, then for hosting and support. The code, the data and the server accounts belong to you.

There is a middle ground too: start on a packaged product, then build small custom tools around it (a customer portal, a reporting dashboard) where it falls short.

When off-the-shelf is the right call

  • You run a small fleet with a standard workflow. If your work is collections, deliveries and daily checks, a mature product already does that well.
  • You need it running this month. A subscription can be live in days. A custom build takes weeks to a few months for a first version.
  • Nobody in the business wants to own a software project. A bespoke system needs someone to make decisions, test releases and give feedback.
  • Your monthly fees are small. If the subscription is a minor line in your costs, building to save on it rarely pays back.

If most of these describe you, buy. Try two or three products with your own drivers before you commit, because driver adoption decides whether any TMS works.

When bespoke pays off

  • Per-vehicle or per-user fees keep climbing. As the fleet grows, a fixed build cost starts to look better than a bill that grows with every truck and driver.
  • You are stitching several tools together. One app for jobs, another for walkaround checks, a spreadsheet for MOT dates and WhatsApp for defects means retyping data and gaps where things get missed.
  • Your workflow is unusual. Specialist loads, connected businesses (haulage plus a workshop or a dealership), or customer reporting your current software cannot produce.
  • You want your own data. Exports, custom reports and integrations stop depending on what a vendor chooses to offer.
  • You have already outgrown a product. Operators who tried packaged software and hit its limits usually know exactly what they need, which makes a bespoke build faster and cheaper to scope.

What it costs over time

Do not compare a monthly subscription with a one-off build price. Compare what each costs over three to five years:

  1. Off-the-shelf: monthly fee multiplied by your expected vehicle and user count in each year, plus any add-on modules, setup and training fees.
  2. Bespoke: the build cost, plus hosting, plus a support and improvement budget each year.

Run both with your own fleet numbers and expected growth. If the totals are close, buy: the vendor carries the maintenance. If bespoke is clearly lower, or the packaged software is costing you time every day, building starts to make sense. What a first bespoke version should include

A bespoke TMS does not need to do everything on day one. For the UK haulier we worked with, the first release covered the work that hurt most, and further modules came in phases. A sensible first version usually includes:

  • Jobs and dispatch with vehicle and driver assignment and live status
  • Digital walkaround checks in a driver app, with photo defect reports
  • Defect-to-repair tracking, so every defect has an owner, a status and a sign-off
  • MOT, tax, insurance and inspection reminders for each vehicle
  • Driver document expiry tracking: licence, Driver CPC, medical and tacho card
  • Audit-ready reports for any vehicle and date range

That platform runs on React, Node.js, Express and MySQL, with the same API powering the office dashboard and the React Native driver app, so both always see the same data. It was deployed to the client's own server, and the business pays no per-vehicle licence fees. You can read the full UK haulier TMS case study and the driver app case study.

Questions to ask before you decide

  1. Which three tasks waste the most office time each week today?
  2. How many vehicles, drivers and office users will you have in three years?
  3. Which records does DVSA, a customer or an accreditation scheme ask you for, and how long do they take to produce?
  4. Which systems must the TMS talk to: accounts, telematics, tachograph analysis, customer portals?
  5. Who in the business will own the project and test releases?
  6. If you buy, can you export all your data if you leave? If you build, will you own the code and servers?

If your answers point to buying, buy, and spend your effort on driver adoption. If they point to building, start with a short scoping call and a written list of modules. Read more about what a TMS should do in our TMS guide for UK hauliers and our guide to digital walkaround checks.
