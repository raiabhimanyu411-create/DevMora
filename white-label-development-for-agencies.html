Agency partnership

Add delivery capacity without building a permanent team first

White-label work needs clear roles, dependable hand-offs and respect for the agency’s client relationship.

Agencies often win work that needs more engineering capacity than their current team can cover. A delivery partner can provide implementation support for a scoped project, a specialist module or an overflow period, while the agency remains the client’s main point of contact. Devmora can work under the agency’s brand and follow the agreed communication model.

The arrangement starts with a clear brief: expected deliverables, technical constraints, acceptance criteria, review cadence, timeline and the roles each team owns. We can join internal calls, work from the agency’s project board and provide updates in the format its delivery lead prefers. The goal is to reduce coordination work, not create another supplier the agency must manage blindly.

The best fit is a defined web app, SaaS feature, API integration, React or Node.js build, Shopify customisation or mobile product. We agree which work is visible to the end client and who handles design, project management, QA and support before development begins.

  • React Native mobile apps

How delivery works

Keep the hand-off clear from brief to release

The agency sets the client context; the delivery team works from an agreed scope and shared source of truth.

Before work starts, we review the design files, repository, acceptance criteria, environments and deployment process. We identify dependencies and questions early, then break the work into reviewable tasks. The agency can choose whether Devmora communicates only with its delivery lead or joins client-facing discussions under an agreed white-label role.

During the build, work is committed to the agreed repository, reviewed through pull requests and demonstrated on a staging environment. The agency receives concise written updates, decisions and risks so it can manage the client relationship with current information. We raise scope changes before doing unplanned work.

At handover, the agency receives the agreed code, technical notes, configuration information and open-item list. Ownership, licences, credentials, deployment responsibility and post-launch support are documented. This makes it easier for the agency to continue the work or transfer it to its own team.

Technical fit

Senior-led delivery across the stack

Use the partner where engineering capacity is the bottleneck, with technology and scope agreed up front.

Devmora works with React, Next.js, Node.js, Express, MySQL, AWS, React Native and Shopify. Our public portfolio includes a transport management platform and driver app, SaaS products, e-commerce marketplaces and commercial vehicle dealer websites. That breadth can help agencies delivering custom software to clients with operational workflows.

A white-label engagement can be fixed-scope or ongoing. For fixed work, the agency and Devmora define deliverables, milestones and change control. For ongoing support, we agree capacity, response expectations, task prioritisation and how unused or additional time is handled. Either model should make the agency’s cost and delivery responsibility clear.

We do not assume every project needs a rewrite. We can work within an existing codebase after an initial review, document the areas we touch and propose a staged plan. That can help an agency take over a client project or deliver a new feature without replacing stable systems.

Trust and ownership

Protect the relationship, code and client information

Confidentiality and access are part of delivery design, not paperwork for the end.

We can sign an NDA and work under the agency’s confidentiality requirements. Access is limited to the systems needed for the agreed work, and the project repository remains under the agency or client’s control when requested. The contract should specify whether code is assigned to the client or agency and how third-party licences are handled.

Communication can be direct with the agency’s technical lead, while client-facing identity and meeting participation follow the agreed model. This avoids unexpected contact or unclear ownership of decisions. The agency can review code and staging work before sharing it with its client.

The agency remains responsible for its own client commitments and commercial terms. Devmora provides the engineering scope agreed with the agency, reports blockers and asks for approvals when requirements change. This division keeps delivery transparent while allowing the partner relationship to stay behind the scenes.

Start small

Pilot the partnership on a defined module

A focused first engagement lets both teams test communication and code review before committing to a larger programme.

A practical pilot might be one integration, a set of front-end screens, an admin workflow or a clearly bounded maintenance sprint. The agency provides access, designs, acceptance criteria and a technical contact. Devmora estimates the work, identifies open questions and shares a delivery plan before implementation starts.

At the end of the pilot, both teams can review estimate accuracy, code quality, review speed, communication and handover. If the arrangement works, the next phase can add capacity or move into an ongoing monthly delivery model. If the project needs are different, the agency still has a clear, documented hand-off.

To discuss a fit, share the type of client work, stack, likely start date and how you want the white-label arrangement to operate. We will respond with questions and a scope rather than a generic rate card.
