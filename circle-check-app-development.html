Canadian fleet workflow

Bring circle checks and defect follow-up into one flow

Make the driver task clear, then make the result useful to dispatch and maintenance.

Canadian drivers and operators may refer to a daily trip inspection as a circle check or pre-trip inspection. A custom app can put the operator’s selected inspection schedule on a phone, let a driver identify the vehicle and trailer, record the inspection result, and report a defect to the office with optional photo evidence.

The app should not treat every fleet as identical. A truck and trailer combination, a bus, a tow truck and a smaller commercial vehicle may follow different schedules and workflows. The checklist, defect categories and required acknowledgements should be configured for the vehicle and the jurisdiction rather than copied from a generic template.

An office dashboard can show submitted reports, unresolved defects, who has been notified and what repair action was recorded. Supervisors can search by unit, driver or date and export the history for the carrier’s operational records. Drivers receive a clear confirmation that their report has reached the office.

  • Vehicle and trailer selection
  • Inspection schedules by vehicle group
  • Defect and no-defect reporting
  • Photo evidence and notes
  • Office review and repair status
  • Records searchable by unit, driver and date
Jurisdiction matters

Configure the app for the province or territory

Canada’s National Safety Code provides shared standards, while implementation and operational requirements are administered through jurisdictions.

Transport Canada describes the National Safety Code as a set of standards used as a basis for commercial vehicle, driver and motor-carrier rules. NSC Standard 13 concerns trip inspection. The precise rules and adopted schedules can vary by province or territory, so the product should be configured for the carrier’s operating jurisdictions.

Ontario, for example, describes daily inspections commonly known as pre-trip inspections or circle checks for specified commercial vehicles. Its guidance calls for following the applicable inspection schedule, recording defects and notifying the operator. Those details are useful for an Ontario deployment; they should not be presented as a universal rule for every Canadian province.

During discovery, we identify where the vehicles operate, which schedules the carrier follows, what records must be carried or stored and who handles defect escalation. The inspection workflow can then be configured around those answers and reviewed by the operator’s safety or compliance lead.

Useful product details

Make the app work for drivers and the back office

The design should reduce re-entry and leave a clear record of the hand-off.

For drivers, a short guided flow is easier to use than a long electronic form. The app can group inspection items by vehicle area, show progress, preserve a draft if the phone loses signal and make a serious defect action clear. With permission, a photo can help explain a defect without replacing the written description.

For the office, reports can flow into a review queue. A supervisor can assign a repair, add a work-order reference and record the close-out. If the same vehicle has several reports, the system can keep each inspection separate while presenting an asset-level history that helps identify repeat issues.

The product can be bilingual where the fleet needs English and French, and it can accommodate multiple depots, roles and vehicle types. Those features should be planned with actual users and local terminology; they should not be added as a generic checkbox after the core workflow is fixed.

If your fleet already has an operations system, we can first assess whether it has an API or export path. If it does not, the inspection app can be scoped as a module in a custom fleet platform with clear ownership of the code and data.

Build decision

Custom development is for workflows that do not fit

Start with the operating gap, not a predetermined feature list.

A packaged inspection product can be a good fit when its schedules, languages, integrations and reporting already match your carrier. A custom build is worth considering when you need different checks by asset, a specific defect-to-repair process, offline use, bilingual workflows or integration with dispatch and maintenance tools.

A discovery call maps one inspection from start to close-out, including the people and systems involved. We then define the first release, which might include the driver app, supervisor review queue, basic reports and one integration. Later releases can add reminders, document tracking or a wider TMS when the first workflow is adopted.

The proposal sets out assumptions, responsibilities, hosting, milestones and data ownership. The carrier remains responsible for validating the final checklists against applicable provincial requirements. The software supports the process that the carrier approves.
