The daily workflow

Move walkaround checks from paper into a usable driver app

A good check app makes the right action quick for drivers and visible to the people who need to respond.

Paper forms are familiar, but a completed sheet can be difficult to find when a vehicle changes driver, a defect needs attention or a manager needs to check what happened. A custom walkaround check app gives drivers a repeatable flow on a phone: choose the vehicle, work through the relevant inspection points, record a nil check or describe a defect, then send the result to the office.

The interface should fit the work, not make the driver learn a complicated system. Large tap targets, short labels, a clear progress state and support for poor connectivity all matter in a depot or roadside setting. If a defect needs a photo, the driver can capture it in context and attach it to the vehicle record instead of sending a separate message that loses its history.

For the office, each submitted check can be tied to a vehicle, driver and time. A defect can become a task for a named person, with an assessment, repair note and close-out status. Managers can see what is waiting, what has been resolved and which vehicle needs a decision before it is assigned again.

  • Checklists tailored to vehicle types
  • Nil-defect confirmation and defect reporting
  • Photo evidence linked to the report
  • Office notifications and repair follow-up
  • Searchable records by vehicle, driver and date
UK context

Design the records around DVSA guidance

Software can support the operator’s process. It does not replace the driver’s responsibilities or the maintenance system.

DVSA guidance says drivers should carry out a walkaround check before a journey and record and report defects. The maintenance guide also describes a system for recording defects and how they were rectified. A digital workflow can make those steps easier to follow by collecting the vehicle identifier, date, defect description, reporter and person notified, then keeping repair details with the report.

The exact checklist should reflect the vehicle and operation. A rigid, identical list can be a poor fit for a mixed fleet with trailers, specialist equipment or different working environments. We can configure the check flow by vehicle class, show relevant items, and allow the operator to update its own inspection prompts as procedures change.

The implementation should preserve a usable history and support the retention and export requirements that apply to the operator. A manager should be able to find the original report and its repair record without depending on an individual driver’s phone. This is an operational design goal; the operator remains responsible for setting and reviewing its roadworthiness procedures.

How a build works

Start with the inspection process, then scope the software

A short discovery phase helps separate essential driver tasks from useful office automation.

We begin by mapping a normal shift: how drivers receive a vehicle, which checks they perform, how they report a fault, who assesses it and how a repair is signed off. We also identify exceptions such as a trailer swap, a vehicle change, a repeated defect or an inspection completed by a responsible person before the driver takes over.

From that map we define the first release: mobile screens, vehicle and driver records, offline behaviour, notifications, office dashboard, exports and integrations. We document what is included, the milestones and the assumptions before development begins. A first version can focus on the walkaround and defect loop, with other TMS modules added when the team is ready.

The app can integrate with an existing fleet database or form part of a wider custom transport management system. Data ownership, hosting, user roles and handover are agreed at the outset. The goal is a workflow your team can operate and maintain, rather than a demo that only works in a presentation.

Fit and alternatives

When a custom walkaround app makes sense

A custom build is most useful when the inspection flow needs to connect to the way your fleet already operates.

A standard fleet product may be the right option when its checklist, reporting and integrations already match your operation. Custom development is worth exploring when drivers switch between several tools, defects are copied manually into office spreadsheets, your vehicle mix needs different flows, or a report must trigger an existing repair or dispatch process.

The decision should include more than the screen design. Consider how many vehicle types need separate checks, whether the app must work offline, who maintains the inspection templates, what records need to be exported and how data moves into the system used by your transport office. These answers shape cost and delivery time more than choosing a particular framework.

If a packaged tool already covers the required workflow at a sensible cost, we will say so. If it does not, we can scope a focused app and show what to build first. Start with the custom TMS development page for the broader fleet platform.
